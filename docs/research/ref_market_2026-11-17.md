## 2. Proposals for Eddington 1919

All proposals and costs below are **INFERENCE**, using your supplied game context.

1. **Complete one compact prediction–measurement–revision loop.**  
   **Player:** commits a prediction, captures usable or ambiguous plates, measures star shifts, sees their own number and scatter, then receives Ashby’s evidence-specific response.  
   **Changes:** Príncipe through the first darkroom interpretation. Target **30–45 minutes as a design hypothesis**, not a market-derived optimum.  
   **Why:** deduction comparators reward player-owned conclusions; Odyssey and We. The Revolution warn against supplied answers and ignored decisions.  
   **Cost:** medium. **Risk:** forcing every result toward relativity would invalidate the experiment. Ambiguous evidence must justify withholding judgment; later Sobral evidence can advance the argument.

2. **Make totality an earned release that preserves observation.**  
   **Player:** rehearses instrument use, works through cloud gaps, then gets a clear opportunity to look up as the engraving becomes blue paper and white corona. Small silhouettes establish scale; silence and music mark the transition.  
   **Changes:** exposure sequence and eclipse climax.  
   **Why:** Before Your Eyes connects interaction to meaning; A Short Hike concentrates experience around a destination. Your epic-climax preference supplies the emotional objective.  
   **Cost:** medium. **Risk:** spectacle or instrument demands could prevent the player from actually witnessing totality.

3. **Keep the evidence together, readable in all four languages.**  
   **Player:** compares plate, micrometer setting, prediction and uncertainty without repeatedly leaving the workbench; can magnify photographic evidence and inspect concise definitions.  
   **Changes:** darkroom interface and translated scientific dialogue. Test whether “supports,” “rules out” and “inconclusive” retain their distinctions in ja/zh-TW/ko.  
   **Why:** Chants’ terminology complaint and Lorelei’s interface complaints expose avoidable barriers to reasoning.  
   **Cost:** medium, including human language review. **Risk:** an overfilled screen obscures the comparison it should clarify.

4. **Teach instrument operation while leaving the conclusion open.**  
   **Player:** practices alignment on a known reference; optional hints explain the next operation without naming the winning theory. Long historical explanations remain available on request. Measurement progress survives interruption.  
   **Changes:** calibration, hints, exposition and darkroom resume behavior.  
   **Why:** Odyssey shows the cost of text walls and highlighted answers; The Occupation shows the cost of lost investigative work.  
   **Cost:** low–medium. **Risk:** excessive hints could turn measurement into obedient clicking.

## 3. Top pick

**Proposal 1: finish the complete evidence loop first.** It tests the Owner’s central promise directly. A player should be able to explain **which observation challenged their prediction, how uncertainty affects the conclusion, and why their confidence changed—or did not**. That is the strongest foundation for the eclipse’s emotional payoff.
