# Sobral Expedition Research Dossier

Sources checked 2026-11-26 JST

**INFERENCE — The chapter can turn on two discoveries made at the same observing station.** During totality, a break in the clouds lets both observers complete their exposure sequences. In the darkroom, Davidson’s wide-field plates show more stars but have blurred images, while Crommelin’s auxiliary instrument yields the sharper evidence. The July return gives the player a second task with a different objective: reproduce the geometry of the original observations closely enough to measure a displacement. Leocádio Araújo’s timing calls, the workers who built the station, and the Brazilian commission’s solar programme all belong in the action. ### 1. People

- **HISTORICAL_RECORD — Andrew Claude de la Cherois Crommelin.** The Irish-born, Cambridge-trained Greenwich astronomer had observed several eclipses and worked with Philip Cowell on predicting Halley’s comet’s return. He replaced the Jesuit astronomer Aloysius Cortie, who could not obtain leave from Stonyhurst. Cortie’s 4-inch lens still went to Sobral, where Crommelin operated it. The British Astronomical Association’s obituary remembers Crommelin as considerate in discussion, direct about errors, encouraging to younger astronomers, and attentive to giving credit. Those are colleagues’ recollections. His own published account supplies an example of his voice: he recalled that some people at the station expected Cortie because word of his withdrawal had failed to reach Sobral, and that the welcome was readily extended to them. [BAA obituary](https://britastro.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/A.%20Crommelin.pdf) · [Cortie correspondence and committee minutes](https://repositorio.iscte-iul.pt/bitstream/10071/34422/1/article_%20110703) · [Crommelin’s account quoted by Crispino and Lima](https://www.scielo.br/j/rbef/a/tsCDFzLWszcgV8XzMN9KvQH/?ilang=en&lang=en)
- **HISTORICAL_RECORD — Charles Rundle Davidson.** A Suffolk-born career Greenwich observer, Davidson entered as a teenage human computer and was a junior assistant during the expedition. Greenwich’s staff record credits him with designing and supervising eclipse apparatus and singles out his skill at adjusting and testing optics. He operated the Sobral astrograph, had overhauled the 4-inch apparatus, and later measured the Sobral plates with Herbert Henry Furner under Frank Dyson. Davidson co-authored the formal report. These sources establish his technical responsibilities and reputation. The field notebook documents the team’s conduct without securely assigning each diary entry to Davidson or Crommelin. [Greenwich staff record](https://www.royalobservatorygreenwich.org/articles.php?article=1288) · [Sobral notebook and instrument assignments](https://www.scielo.br/j/rbef/a/kr4wVYLHqLSVyyHWmKpWdNv/?lang=pt) · [Press history and the auxiliary instrument](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/00732753261419603) · [Kennefick’s archival account](https://physicstoday.aip.org/features/testing-relativity-from-the-1919-eclipse-a-question-of-bias)
- **HISTORICAL_RECORD — Their public voice.** Both men signed explanations of the experiment for Brazilian readers. In their joint return interview they named the people who had helped them, explained the micrometer, declined to lend uncopied glass originals for reproduction, and reserved judgment on Einstein until the plates could be measured. That public exchange gives a scene writer firmer footing than assigning separate private convictions to the two men. [*Estado do Pará* front page](https://hemeroteca-pdf.bn.gov.br/800082/per800082_1919_02898.pdf) · [Return interview transcribed by Crispino and Lima](https://www.scielo.br/j/rbef/a/TLy8ScmGq3wY5fWHYKdm76F/?lang=pt)
- **HISTORICAL_RECORD — Hosts and helpers.** Leocádio Araújo, an engineer in the Ceará agricultural administration, interpreted, helped with preparations, translated the pair’s Sobral newspaper article, and ran the metronome during totality. At Camocim, **John Nicolau**, as named in the British report, helped get the baggage through to Sobral. Prefect and physician **José Jácome de Oliveira** and **Monsignor Ferreira** headed the civic and ecclesiastical reception at the station. Federal deputy **Colonel Vicente Saboya de Albuquerque** lent the house used by the British and later by the Americans. Local workers built instrument supports and shelters. The report names **Nicolau and Carneiro** as the people entrusted with forwarding the British packing cases. [British report, §§9–15](https://w.astro.berkeley.edu/~kalas/labs/documents/dyson1920.pdf) · [Royal Society letter to the Brazilian ambassador](https://catalogues.royalsociety.org/CalmView/Record.aspx?id=NLB%2F57%2F541&src=CalmView.Catalog) · [Study of expedition labour](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/british-journal-for-the-history-of-science/article/british-and-brazilian-expeditions-and-the-1919-total-solar-eclipse-regimes-of-labour-and-degrees-of-invisibility/B41C74597F46C6A7A3C5406DC84AF5DB)
- **HISTORICAL_RECORD — Henrique Morize and the Observatório Nacional.** Morize, the Rio-based director, recommended Sobral after considering weather and transport, drawing on telegraph engineer **Benjamin de Oliveira’s** survey. He pursued federal funding, arranged transport and accommodation, and led a Brazilian programme on the **shape and structure of the solar corona and its spectrum**. The commission included astronomers **Domingos Fernandes da Costa** and **Allyrio de Mattos**, mathematician and calculator **Lélio Gama**, chemist **Theophilo Lee**, meteorologist **Luiz Rodrigues**, mechanic **Arthur de Castro Almeida**, and carpenter **Primo Flores**. The Brazilian camp at Praça do Patrocínio had its own photographic instruments and spectrographs. A surviving Mailhat corona image is credited to Allyrio de Mattos. The Brazilian commission also gathered weather data used by the British and left equipment for a local meteorological station. The Carnegie pair, **Daniel Maynard Wise** and **Andrew Thomson**, had a separate programme on terrestrial magnetism and atmospheric effects. [Morize’s programme and report excerpts](https://www.gov.br/observatorio/pt-br/assuntos/biblioteca/imagem/OEclipsede1919_ON.pdf) · [Brazilian team roster](https://www.sbhc.org.br/conteudo/view?id_conteudo=1073) · [Brazilian plate study](https://www.scielo.br/j/hcsm/a/3cQFXzYr5Fs8V7yKMVtzgQc/?lang=pt) · [Brazilian and American logistics](https://repositorio.iscte-iul.pt/bitstream/10071/34422/1/article_%20110703)

**HISTORICAL_RECORD — Exposure counts.** Davidson’s 13-inch astrograph, stopped to 8 inches and fed by a 16-inch coelostat, exposed **19 plates**, alternating 5 and 10 seconds. Several showed 12 stars and 16 entered the quoted reduction. Crommelin’s 4-inch, 19-foot-focus instrument, fed by a separate 8-inch coelostat, exposed **eight plates at 28 seconds each**. Seven plates each gave seven measurable star images. Plate 6 showed a prominence through cloud and no stars. The notebook’s proposed 30-second exposures for the small instrument are rehearsal figures. [British report, §§14, 16 and 25](https://w.astro.berkeley.edu/~kalas/labs/documents/dyson1920.pdf) · [Field notebook programme](https://www.scielo.br/j/rbef/a/kr4wVYLHqLSVyyHWmKpWdNv/?lang=pt)

**INFERENCE — Clock for the scene.** The report’s assumed second contact, **1919-05-28 23:58:18 astronomical GMT**, converts to **1919-05-29 11:58:18 civil GMT**, or **1919-05-29 08:58:18 Sobral local (GMT−03:00)**. Use that as the exposure sequence’s synchronization point. The observers timed exposures from second contact with a metronome that made 320 beats in 310 seconds, then corrected the recorded exposure times. They did not independently clock the contact to the second. [British report, §§13–14](https://w.astro.berkeley.edu/~kalas/labs/documents/dyson1920.pdf) · [NIST on the noon-based astronomical day](https://www.nist.gov/blogs/taking-measure/everyday-time-and-atomic-time-part-4)

### 3. Ceará, Sobral, and the press

- **HISTORICAL_RECORD — A railway town in a drought.** Sobral was a regional agricultural trading centre with cotton processing and a textile mill. The Camocim railway linked that trade to the coast. The town had a municipal market, gas lighting arriving around the eclipse period, a theatre that showed films, social clubs, and a racecourse where racing and betting were suspended for the observations. The drought drove *retirantes* from the interior toward towns including Sobral and Fortaleza, and some families left Ceará for the Amazon or farther south. On 1919-05-07 Sobral local, *A Lucta* reported Saboya’s telegram about drought-relief works, including dams, roads, and railway construction. The British report records the contrast at house level: water was generally scarce, while the expedition’s lodging had ample piped water for photography. [MAST town history and period photographs](https://www.gov.br/observatorio/pt-br/assuntos/biblioteca/imagem/EclipseSobral_DigitalLivro_Completo.pdf) · [*A Lucta*, drought-relief dispatch](https://hemeroteca-pdf.bn.gov.br/720763/per720763_1919_00263.pdf) · [British report, §§11–12](https://w.astro.berkeley.edu/~kalas/labs/documents/dyson1920.pdf)
- **HISTORICAL_RECORD — Influenza and yellow fever are separate threads.** Belém’s documented influenza wave ran from October through December 1918 Belém local, before the *Anselm* arrived. Ceará’s press covered influenza in Camocim on 1918-10-27 Ceará local and in Sobral on 1919-08-13 Ceará local. The health scare specifically linked to expedition preparations in the archival scholarship was **yellow fever** around Sobral. It prompted discussions about mosquito protection and measures at the British lodging. [UFPA archival study of influenza in Belém](https://repositorio.ufpa.br/items/1204b1f2-3316-4a33-88a5-9c7c73a95540) · [Ceará influenza study and newspaper references](https://revistas.uece.br/index.php/centurias/article/view/11018) · [Morize’s preparations and yellow fever](https://repositorio.iscte-iul.pt/bitstream/10071/34422/1/article_%20110703)
- **HISTORICAL_RECORD — Hospitality and disagreement shared the page.** On 1919-04-20 Belém local, *Estado do Pará* ran the visitors’ explanation of the light-bending experiment. On 1919-04-22 Belém local, writer **Paulino de Brito** answered that discovering how to bring rain to drought-stricken people would matter more to them than settling the path of starlight. In Sobral, the Catholic *Correio da Semana* welcomed the visitors and printed their article in Araújo’s translation on 1919-05-24 Sobral local. The opposition *A Lucta* also covered the expedition and highlighted the Americans’ work. *A Ordem* interviewed Morize about both the eclipse and the prospects for weather forecasting. In Camocim, *Folha do Littoral* reprinted the British explanation on 1919-05-11 Ceará local. [British article](https://hemeroteca-pdf.bn.gov.br/800082/per800082_1919_02898.pdf) · [Brito’s reply](https://hemeroteca-pdf.bn.gov.br/800082/per800082_1919_02900.pdf) · [*Correio da Semana* facsimile](https://www.gov.br/observatorio/pt-br/assuntos/biblioteca/imagem/PELOCEUDESOBRAL_ON.pdf) · [*Folha do Littoral* facsimile](https://hemeroteca-pdf.bn.gov.br/800198/per800198_1919_00047.pdf) · [Carolino and Simões’s newspaper study](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/00732753261419603)
- **HISTORICAL_RECORD — Public science and national pride.** Shops closed for the eclipse, two telescopes were provided for public viewing, and a correspondent described people gathering in squares with smoked glass. Morize distributed explanatory material and made weather observation a continuing local activity. The Brazilian embassy’s letter to the Royal Society expressed national pride in the Sobral observations. Brazilian coverage was substantial around the eclipse itself. The later London relativity announcement received much thinner coverage in the Brazilian papers surveyed by the Observatório Nacional. [*A Noite* eclipse dispatch](https://hemeroteca-pdf.bn.gov.br/348970/per348970_1919_02679.pdf) · [Newspaper study](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/00732753261419603) · [Brazilian embassy letter, Royal Society catalogue](https://catalogues.royalsociety.org/calmview/Record.aspx?AddBasket=MC%2F34%2F14&id=MC%2F34%2F14&src=CalmView.Catalog) · [Observatório Nacional, press and public science](https://www.gov.br/observatorio/pt-br/assuntos/biblioteca/imagem/OEclipsede1919_ON.pdf)

### 4. Instruments and playable decisions

| Moment | Documented decision and plausible player action |
|---|---|
| **Build and focus** | **HISTORICAL_RECORD.** The coelostats used clock-driven mirrors to feed fixed horizontal tubes. The small drive had to be reversed for the Southern Hemisphere. Test exposures on Arcturus at different settings revealed astigmatism in the big mirror. An 8-inch stop reduced it. The small camera’s plate was turned 45 degrees to fit seven stars. **INFERENCE.** Let the player bracket focus, inspect star shapes, and see how stopping down or rotating the plate changes the usable field. [British report, §10](https://w.astro.berkeley.edu/~kalas/labs/documents/dyson1920.pdf) |
| **Partial phase and totality** | **HISTORICAL_RECORD.** Cloud gaps gave a short opportunity to centre the field and trim the drives. The larger drive ran unevenly, which favoured short exposures. The team chose its plate stocks and schedules during preparation, then carried them out during totality. **INFERENCE.** Give the player a brief alignment window, then expose and change holders to Araújo’s cadence at one station. Run the other station in parallel. Score cadence, tracking, and useful images. A clouded plate can still show the corona or a prominence. [Field notebook](https://www.scielo.br/j/rbef/a/kr4wVYLHqLSVyyHWmKpWdNv/?lang=pt) · [British report, §§12–14](https://w.astro.berkeley.edu/~kalas/labs/documents/dyson1920.pdf) |
| **Darkroom and return** | **HISTORICAL_RECORD.** Heat made some emulsions difficult to develop. The team used earthenware water coolers and worked at night or before dawn. The astrograph’s softness became clear after development. July work required remounting the mirrors in their marked positions and finding the same field at roughly the original altitude. **INFERENCE.** Let the player inspect sharpness and star count separately, log the defect before any deflection estimate appears, and reproduce the field before dawn in July. Keep the historical mirror blur as a fault to diagnose. The record does not establish a quick field fix. [British report, §§12–17 and 23](https://w.astro.berkeley.edu/~kalas/labs/documents/dyson1920.pdf) |
| **Príncipe counterpart** | **HISTORICAL_RECORD.** At Sundy, Eddington and Cottingham used one astrograph. They exposed 16 plates after a morning storm and under drifting cloud. Seven recorded stars and two supplied the adopted result. Their reduction used pre-expedition Oxford comparison plates of the eclipse field and check-field plates linking Oxford and Príncipe. At Sobral, both instruments ran, and the July comparison plates came from the original station. **INFERENCE.** Make Príncipe play around getting enough starlight through cloud. Make Sobral play around getting two instruments through a fixed sequence, then discovering that an apparently successful exposure can still be weak evidence. [British report, §§26–32 and 39](https://w.astro.berkeley.edu/~kalas/labs/documents/dyson1920.pdf) · [Kennefick on the comparison methods](https://physicstoday.aip.org/features/testing-relativity-from-the-1919-eclipse-a-question-of-bias) |

### 5. Documented tensions and the weighting controversy

- **HISTORICAL_RECORD — The defect preceded the result.** The big mirror had shown astigmatism in rehearsal, yet the stopped-down system had good focus on the night of 1919-05-27 Sobral local. The eclipse images were diffuse, the mechanical setting was unchanged on rechecking, and the predawn July images were sharp again. The observers attributed the temporary defect to uneven solar heating of the coelostat mirror. They could not establish whether it changed the effective image scale or merely blurred the star images. A scale change moves image positions progressively with distance from the plate centre and can be hard to distinguish from the small outward displacement being measured. [British report, §§23–25](https://w.astro.berkeley.edu/~kalas/labs/documents/dyson1920.pdf) · [Kennefick’s diagrams and analysis](https://physicstoday.aip.org/features/testing-relativity-from-the-1919-eclipse-a-question-of-bias)
- **HISTORICAL_RECORD — Three series, three evidentiary weights.** The formal report gives **1.98″ ± 0.12″ probable error** for the Sobral 4-inch and **1.61″ ± 0.30″ probable error** for Príncipe. It publishes **0.93″** for the Sobral astrograph using the larger fitted scale change, and **1.52″** using only the scale term expected from refraction and aberration. The authors gave the discordant series little weight and the 4-inch series the most weight, citing image quality, larger image scale, and agreement between measurements in the two coordinates. The stated alternatives at the solar limb were zero, about **0.87″** for the Newtonian-style calculation, and **1.75″** for general relativity. [British report, §§2, 23–25 and 39](https://w.astro.berkeley.edu/~kalas/labs/documents/dyson1920.pdf) · [Gilmore and Tausch-Pebody on probable error](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/items/b89ca158-7c72-41aa-9c63-65785e31ac94)
- **HISTORICAL_RECORD — Who made the choice.** Davidson and Furner measured Sobral at Greenwich under Dyson. Eddington measured and reduced Príncipe at Cambridge. Eddington’s reply to Dyson of 1919-10-03 UK local discusses his own provisional and revised scale treatment. Kennefick uses that correspondence and the Greenwich worksheets to place the Sobral weighting decision with Dyson’s group. Dyson’s later letter to Frank Schlesinger says the result ran contrary to his expectations. Crommelin’s contemporary *Nature* account acknowledged the possibility of systematic error in the astrograph series. [Kennefick, archival quotations and worksheets](https://physicstoday.aip.org/features/testing-relativity-from-the-1919-eclipse-a-question-of-bias) · [Crommelin, *Nature* results account](https://www.nature.com/articles/104280a0) · [Kennefick, *No Shadow of a Doubt*](https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691183862/no-shadow-of-a-doubt)
- **HISTORICAL_RECORD — Later disagreement.** In 1979 UK publication year, Geoffrey Harvey reported a Greenwich remeasurement using a modern plate-measuring machine and astrometric software. Kennefick gives **1.90″ ± 0.11″** for the 4-inch and **1.55″ ± 0.34″** for the astrograph. The remeasurement used a somewhat different selection of plates and stars. In 1980 US publication year, John Earman and Clark Glymour argued that the low astrograph result deserved inclusion and challenged the strength of the original conclusion. Gilmore and Tausch-Pebody subsequently showed that their comparison treated internal scatter as the uncertainty on a mean and failed to account for the astrograph’s systematic error. [Harvey’s *Observatory* record](https://scixplorer.org/abs/1979Obs....99..195H/abstract) · [Kennefick’s results table](https://physicstoday.aip.org/features/testing-relativity-from-the-1919-eclipse-a-question-of-bias) · [Earman and Glymour’s paper](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/philosophy/docs/glymour/relativityeclipses.pdf) · [Gilmore and Tausch-Pebody’s reassessment](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/items/b89ca158-7c72-41aa-9c63-65785e31ac94)
- **INFERENCE — Where to put the tension.** Let the darkroom notebook establish the fault while the observers still have no result. In a Greenwich scene, the player can compare what happens under two scale assumptions and see why the blur matters. The documented public confrontation in Brazil is Brito’s drought criticism. The scientific dispute over weighting belongs to the reduction and its later reception. The sources support both without supplying a Crommelin–Davidson feud or an order from Eddington to suppress a result. [Brito’s column](https://hemeroteca-pdf.bn.gov.br/800082/per800082_1919_02900.pdf) · [Field notebook](https://www.scielo.br/j/rbef/a/kr4wVYLHqLSVyyHWmKpWdNv/?lang=pt) · [Kennefick’s archival account](https://physicstoday.aip.org/features/testing-relativity-from-the-1919-eclipse-a-question-of-bias)

| Sobral had a cloudless eclipse, or its bad plates were all blank. | **HISTORICAL_RECORD.** Cloud obscured first contact and crossed the Sun during totality. | A visibly cracked or permanently warped mirror was discovered during totality. | **HISTORICAL_RECORD.** The defect emerged during plate inspection. ## Sources

- **Primary British records.** The [formal expedition report](https://w.astro.berkeley.edu/~kalas/labs/documents/dyson1920.pdf) supplies routes, apparatus, exposure and comparison tables, reduction, and weighting. The [Sobral notebook in Crispino’s annotated transcription](https://www.scielo.br/j/rbef/a/kr4wVYLHqLSVyyHWmKpWdNv/?lang=pt) supplies construction dates, rehearsal programmes, and the first assessment of blur. [*Nature*’s telegram notice](https://www.nature.com/articles/103265c0) and [Crommelin’s results account](https://www.nature.com/articles/104280a0) show what was reported at each stage. Crommelin’s [*Observatory* field narrative, vol. 42, pp. 368–371](https://adsbit.harvard.edu/full/1919Obs....42..368C/0000368.000.html) is cited through the [scholarly account that quotes it](https://www.scielo.br/j/rbef/a/tsCDFzLWszcgV8XzMN9KvQH/?ilang=en&lang=en).
- **Brazilian institutional records and scholarship.** [MAST’s *O Eclipse de Sobral*](https://www.gov.br/mast/pt-br/imagens/publicacoes/2021/livro-eclipse-sobral-digital.pdf) reproduces documents and covers the interpreters, programme, town, and July work. The [Observatório Nacional volume](https://www.gov.br/observatorio/pt-br/assuntos/biblioteca/imagem/OEclipsede1919_ON.pdf) excerpts Morize’s results and surveys reception. [Barboza’s plate study](https://www.scielo.br/j/hcsm/a/3cQFXzYr5Fs8V7yKMVtzgQc/?lang=pt) identifies Brazilian images and techniques. [Carolino and Simões](https://repositorio.iscte-iul.pt/bitstream/10071/34422/1/article_%20110703) document site selection, funding, and logistical correspondence. [Simões, Soares, and Carolino](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/british-journal-for-the-history-of-science/article/british-and-brazilian-expeditions-and-the-1919-total-solar-eclipse-regimes-of-labour-and-degrees-of-invisibility/B41C74597F46C6A7A3C5406DC84AF5DB) document local labour.
- **Brazilian press.** Hemeroteca scans support the [British explanation in *Estado do Pará*](https://hemeroteca-pdf.bn.gov.br/800082/per800082_1919_02898.pdf), [Paulino de Brito’s reply](https://hemeroteca-pdf.bn.gov.br/800082/per800082_1919_02900.pdf), [*A Lucta* on drought relief](https://hemeroteca-pdf.bn.gov.br/720763/per720763_1919_00263.pdf), [*Folha do Littoral*’s coverage](https://hemeroteca-pdf.bn.gov.br/800198/per800198_1919_00047.pdf), and [*A Noite*’s eclipse dispatch](https://hemeroteca-pdf.bn.gov.br/348970/per348970_1919_02679.pdf). The [Observatório Nacional facsimile](https://www.gov.br/observatorio/pt-br/assuntos/biblioteca/imagem/PELOCEUDESOBRAL_ON.pdf) preserves *Correio da Semana*. [Crispino and Lima](https://www.scielo.br/j/rbef/a/TLy8ScmGq3wY5fWHYKdm76F/?lang=pt) transcribe the return interview. [Carolino and Simões](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/00732753261419603) index the wider press record, including *A Ordem* and reports on public viewing.
- **Reduction and reassessment.** [Kennefick’s archival article](https://physicstoday.aip.org/features/testing-relativity-from-the-1919-eclipse-a-question-of-bias) provides the correspondence, division of labour, and Harvey comparison table. [*No Shadow of a Doubt*](https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691183862/no-shadow-of-a-doubt) is his book-length treatment. [Harvey](https://scixplorer.org/abs/1979Obs....99..195H/abstract), [Earman and Glymour](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/philosophy/docs/glymour/relativityeclipses.pdf), and [Gilmore and Tausch-Pebody](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/items/b89ca158-7c72-41aa-9c63-65785e31ac94) identify the original remeasurement, the critique, and the later statistical reassessment.

## Unsure

- I found joint public statements and Crommelin’s published recollections, but no separately attributable private Sobral letter from Davidson in the sources examined. Woolley’s memoir was catalogued, but the publisher call `web.open https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rsbm.1971.0007` returned HTTP 403. The permitted alternatives were the Royal Society catalogue, Greenwich’s staff record, and the field notebook. They support the documented work and reputation above, with limited evidence for a private temperament.
