# Society and Influenza, 1918–1920

Sources checked 2026-11-26 JST

The strongest additions are Cambridge’s school-closure reporting in **I1**, Funchal’s port-health coverage in **The Madeira wait**, Sobral’s influenza clipping in **I3**, and a sequence of sharply different headlines and illustrations in **I5–I6**. The principal evidence gap is influenza on São Tomé and Príncipe itself. Cabo Verde supplies documented context for **I2**, where the expedition actually called.

**Currency and dating.** Sources checked as of **26 November 2026 JST**. Newspaper dates are local publication dates, legal dates are local civil dates, and figures are identified as contemporary counts or later estimates. `HISTORICAL_RECORD` identifies what a record establishes, including what a newspaper *claimed*. `INFERENCE` identifies the proposed screen placement. A dated archival insert establishes publication, not receipt by an expedition member.

### 1. Influenza along the journey

- **Britain.** `HISTORICAL_RECORD` The official English and Welsh returns show three waves, in summer **1918 UK local time**, autumn **1918 UK local time**, and late winter into spring **1919 UK local time**. The Registrar-General attributed **151,446 deaths** to influenza over the 46 weeks from **23 June 1918 to 10 May 1919 UK local time**, about three-quarters in the second wave. This is the contemporary England-and-Wales count for that interval. The British press reported outbreaks and published advice while officials and doctors still disputed diagnosis and prevention. Wartime censorship and uncertainty both shaped the reporting. [Ministry of Health pandemic report, introductory mortality figures](https://wellcomecollection.org/works/puxqa3uh/items?canvas=21) · [Michael Bresalier, *Fighting Flu*](https://pure.manchester.ac.uk/ws/files/24146372/POST-PEER-REVIEW-PUBLISHERS.PDF)
- **Cambridge town and University.** `HISTORICAL_RECORD` The *Cambridge Daily News* on **4 July 1918 Cambridge local time** ran “Cambridge and County Practically Immune” over “Cadets the Worst Sufferers.” By **24 October 1918 Cambridge local time**, the same paper reported closure of the borough’s public elementary schools, with reopening planned for **4 November 1918 Cambridge local time**, and also reported the closure of the County Girls’ and County Boys’ schools. The New Theatre barred schoolchildren while the borough schools were closed. At the University, botanist and St John’s fellow **Reginald Philip Gregory** died at home on **24 November 1918 Cambridge local time**, of pneumonia following influenza, according to A. C. Seward’s contemporary *Nature* obituary. These are borough school measures and an individual University loss. [Cambridge press, summer transcripts](https://capturingcambridge.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/SpanishFluCambridge-Pt1.pdf) · [Cambridge press, autumn transcripts](https://capturingcambridge.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/SpanishFluCambridgePt2.pdf) · [Seward’s obituary, pp. 247–248](https://www.nature.com/articles/102247a0)
- **Cambridge through departure.** `HISTORICAL_RECORD` On **3 December 1918 Cambridge local time**, the *Cambridge Daily News* carried “Bacon-Eating and Influenza: Dr. Laird’s View.” The borough medical officer rejected the rumour that eating bacon caused the outbreak. On **14 March 1919 Cambridge local time**, after the party had sailed, the *Cambridge Independent Press* ran “Schools Closed in Sixty Parishes.” The report concerned **Cambridgeshire**, described closures in about 60 parishes, and noted that the county’s updated leaflet recommended masks for people attending or nursing influenza patients. [Cambridge press, December–March transcripts, PDF pp. 1 and 7–8](https://capturingcambridge.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/SpanishFluCambridge_Pt4.pdf) · [Expedition report, §§8–9](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsta.1920.0009)
- **Liverpool.** `HISTORICAL_RECORD` The city was still in its third wave around departure. A contemporary tabulation gives **196 registered influenza deaths** in the week ending **1 March 1919 Liverpool local time** and **149** in the week ending **8 March 1919 Liverpool local time**, the day *Anselm* sailed. The table was published later. It is evidence for the city’s conditions around embarkation. [*Public Health Reports*, table III, p. 1371](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/67908/cdc_67908_DS1.pdf) · [Expedition report, §9](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsta.1920.0009)
- **Lisbon.** `HISTORICAL_RECORD` Portugal’s second wave was its deadliest, peaking in autumn **1918 Portugal local time**. Lisbon’s historical study reports **3,843 deaths attributed to influenza** in **October 1918 Lisbon local time**, out of **4,743 deaths from all causes** that month. A government order dated **23 October 1918 Lisbon local time**, published on **25 October 1918 Lisbon local time**, required civil registries to stay open even on Sundays and to obtain the death registers they needed. The order was revoked on **16 November 1918 Lisbon local time**, months before the expedition’s **12 March 1919 Lisbon local time** reception. [Portuguese Parliament epidemic history](https://www.parlamento.pt/Parlamento/Paginas/Epidemias-e-o-Parlamento.aspx) · [Lisbon study and cited *Diário do Governo* orders](https://www.ics.ulisboa.pt/sites/ics.ulisboa.pt/files/Imprensa/pneumonica_-_cap_11.pdf) · [Eddington’s letter, Trinity EDDN/A/4/2](https://archives.trin.cam.ac.uk/index.php/letter-from-a-s-eddington-to-sarah-ann-eddington-12)
- **Madeira and Funchal.** `HISTORICAL_RECORD` Funchal’s *Diário da Madeira*, no. 2304, p. 1, printed “A epidemia e o porto do Funchal” on **16 November 1918 Funchal local time**. No. 2306, p. 1, reported on the Gonçalo Aires isolation hospital on **19 November 1918 Funchal local time**. These are archival issue references traced by a Madeira study. A separate demographic reconstruction estimates **121.14 excess all-cause deaths per 10,000** in Madeira over **1918–1920 Madeira local time**. That estimate covers the district and a longer interval than the expedition’s stay. [Madeira press research, notes 19–25](https://www.academia.edu/83834746/Learning_from_the_past_The_Saborea_project_and_the_rebranding_of_the_Madeira_destination_in_the_post_pandemic_era) · [Nunes et al., regional mortality analysis](https://academic.oup.com/aje/article/187/12/2541/5067655?searchresult=1)
- **São Tomé and Príncipe, with a documented onward port.** `HISTORICAL_RECORD` The expedition report records calls at São Vicente and Santiago in Cabo Verde before landing at Santo António, Príncipe, on **23 April 1919 Príncipe local time**. A Cabo Verde historical compilation lists influenza in **1918 Cabo Verde local time** on Santo Antão, São Vicente, Santiago and Brava, with **7,820 affected and 85 deaths** recorded across the four islands. In that table, **ST means Santiago**. The São Tomé and Príncipe colonial-press catalogue identifies the *Boletim Official* as a search lead. The sources reviewed did not establish an influenza outbreak date, death count, or local closure on either São Tomé or Príncipe. [Expedition report, §26](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsta.1920.0009) · [Cabo Verde historical epidemic table 12 and island key](https://api.eciencia.cv/server/api/core/bitstreams/a2498a4d-8230-4d26-b1c5-2a251c776fe7/content) · [NOVA colonial-press catalogue](https://expoimprensacolonial.fcsh.unl.pt/sao-tome-principe)
- **Pará and Belém.** `HISTORICAL_RECORD` The UFPA study traces early-October **1918 Belém local time** reporting around the steamer *Ceará*, arriving from Rio, and documents discrepancies among mortality sources. The front page of *Estado do Pará* on **28 October 1918 Belém local time** gives a usable contemporary line, “A influenza alastra-se” (“Influenza is spreading”), alongside war dispatches and domestic remedies. [UFPA, *O vírus e a cidade*](https://repositorio.ufpa.br/items/1204b1f2-3316-4a33-88a5-9c7c73a95540) · [*Estado do Pará*, digitised front page](https://hemeroteca-pdf.bn.gov.br/800082/per800082_1918_02725.pdf)
- **Ceará and Sobral.** `HISTORICAL_RECORD` Sobral’s *A Lucta* carried a public-health directorate notice on **23 October 1918 Sobral local time**, “A Peste” on **5 February 1919 Sobral local time**, and “Carnaval versus Grippe” on **23 April 1919 Sobral local time**, one week before the British pair reached Sobral. It ran another item titled “A Gripe” on **13 August 1919 Sobral local time**. These issue references establish that influenza remained a subject of local reporting across the journey. [Batista and Paula, *Dançando com a morte*, notes 19 and 34](https://periodicos.ufes.br/agora/pt_BR/article/download/34051/23604/110310) · [Silva, *A Gripe Espanhola no Ceará*, newspaper references](https://revistas.uece.br/index.php/centurias/article/view/11018) · [Expedition report, §§9–10](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsta.1920.0009)
- **Rio de Janeiro.** `HISTORICAL_RECORD` Goulart’s archival study puts the epidemic’s Rio toll at **about 15,000 deaths** in **1918 Rio local time**. Fiocruz documents emergency medical posts organised with Carlos Chagas’s help. Commercial claims also filled the newspapers. *A Noite* of **20 November 1918 Rio local time** advertised a lung tonic as effective against influenza and a long list of other ailments. The advertisement documents the sales pitch. [Goulart, *Revisitando a espanhola*, pp. 101–142](https://www.scielo.br/j/hcsm/a/Wkqm45R4ptVzTqSpKxJhfRh/?format=pdf&lang=pt) · [Instituto Oswaldo Cruz on the Rio response](https://www.ioc.fiocruz.br/node/1912) · [*A Noite*, digitised issue](https://hemeroteca-pdf.bn.gov.br/348970/per348970_1918_02492.pdf)
- **Expedition members.** `HISTORICAL_RECORD` The records reviewed establish the itinerary, working conditions and operational delays. They did not yield a pandemic influenza diagnosis for Eddington, Cottingham, Crommelin or Davidson. Aloysius Cortie, considered for the Brazil party, could not obtain the time away. His obituary dates his influenza to **April 1925 Britain local time**, outside the journey period. [Trinity Eddington papers, EDDN/A/4 and EDDN/C/1/3](https://archives.trin.cam.ac.uk/downloads/reports/papers-of-sir-arthur-eddington-itemList.html) · [Sobral notebook, RGO 8/166, reproduced by Crispino](https://www.scielo.br/j/rbef/a/kr4wVYLHqLSVyyHWmKpWdNv/?lang=pt) · [Greenwich expedition history and contemporary references](https://www.royalobservatorygreenwich.org/articles.php?article=1283) · [Cortie obituary](https://britastro.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/A%2CL.%20Cortie.pdf)

### 2. Women and post-war Britain

- **Two Acts and the election.** `HISTORICAL_RECORD` The Representation of the People Act received Royal Assent on **6 February 1918 Westminster local time**. It enfranchised about **8.4 million women**, aged **30 or older** and subject to the prescribed qualifications. Section 4 included a university-constituency route for women who had passed the final examination and met the residence requirement at a university that withheld degrees from women. The separate Parliament (Qualification of Women) Act of **21 November 1918 Westminster local time** opened Commons membership to women. Polling took place on **14 December 1918 UK and Ireland local time**. Counting was delayed to **28 December 1918 UK and Ireland local time** to allow overseas service votes to arrive. Seventeen women stood. Constance Markievicz was elected for Dublin St Patrick’s and, as a Sinn Féin member, did not take her Westminster seat. [Representation Act, §4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1918/64/pdfs/ukpga_19180064_en.pdf) · [Commons Library franchise figures](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-8361/) · [Qualification of Women Act](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Geo5/8-9/47) · [UK Parliament on candidates](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/transformingsociety/electionsvoting/womenvote/case-studies-women-parliament/what-difference/the-vote-for-some/) · [Government history of the election and count](https://history.blog.gov.uk/2018/12/14/the-woman-in-green-who-won-the-khaki-election/)
- **Cambridge study and paid astronomy.** `HISTORICAL_RECORD` Cambridge rejected titles of degrees for women by **1,713 to 662** on **21 May 1897 Cambridge local time**. The University allowed titles of degrees in **1921 Cambridge local time** and full membership came into effect in **1948 Cambridge local time**. At the Cambridge Observatory, Harriet Hardy was the first woman employed as a computer. Annie Walker did paid computation and observing, working part-time from **1879 Cambridge local time** and full-time from **1882 to 1903 Cambridge local time**. Cecilia Payne entered Newnham in **1919 Cambridge local time** and recalled hearing Eddington explain the eclipse results. Her later account gets the expedition’s year and Eddington’s destination wrong, so use it as evidence of her response to the lecture. [University exhibit and vote tally](https://www.cam.ac.uk/stories/the-rising-tide) · [Newnham’s degree chronology](https://newn.cam.ac.uk/about/history/no-ordinary-rag-the-attack-on-the-clough-gates) · [Cambridge on Hardy and Walker](https://www.cam.ac.uk/stories/asteroid-named-after-first-woman-at-cambridge-paid-to-observe-the-heavens) · [Institute of Astronomy staff history](https://www.ast.cam.ac.uk/about/history/biographies-t-z) · [Payne’s recollection with editorial correction](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Extras/Payne-Gaposchkin_Eddington/)
- **Greenwich and professional recognition.** `HISTORICAL_RECORD` Greenwich recruited mathematically trained women from Girton and Newnham for astrographic work from **1890 Greenwich local time**. Annie Russell, later Maunder, was a Greenwich computer, returned as a wartime volunteer, became a Royal Astronomical Society fellow in **1916 UK local time**, and was formally reinstated as a computer around the end of **September 1919 Greenwich local time**. Greenwich’s institutional history documents those appointments and her solar work. The expedition sources reviewed give no basis for assigning her the Sobral plate reductions. [Royal Society on the Greenwich computers](https://royalsociety.org/blog/2025/08/lady-computers/) · [Greenwich appointment history](https://www.royalobservatorygreenwich.org/articles.php?article=1274) · [Eclipse report, §§16–25](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsta.1920.0009)
- **Demobilisation, jobs and unrest.** `HISTORICAL_RECORD` Demobilisation delays prompted a soldiers’ protest at Folkestone on **3 January 1919 Folkestone local time**. Glasgow’s forty-hour strike brought a mass rally and a clash with police in George Square on **31 January 1919 Glasgow local time**. On **28 February 1919 UK local time**, Employment Exchange live registers listed **549,261 women and 416,150 men**, as reported by the Ministry of Labour. These are register counts. A **14 July 1919 Westminster local time** Commons answer describes the obligation of establishments covered by the restoration measure to end wartime “diluted labour,” male or female, where it departed from pre-war practice, unless an agreement modified the obligation. A surviving Women’s Royal Naval Service report documents the Liverpool division’s involvement in the police strike in **August 1919 Liverpool local time**. [National Army Museum on demobilisation](https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/peace-and-commemoration) · [Glasgow forty-hour strike poster](https://www.theglasgowstory.com/image/?inum=TGSE00886) · [National Records of Scotland, George Square records](https://blog.nrscotland.gov.uk/2019/01/31/voices-from-our-archives-the-george-square-riot/) · [*Labour Gazette*, employment summary](https://escoe-website.s3.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/26114529/The-Ministry-of-Labour-Gazette-Mar-1919.pdf) · [Hansard, “Women (Employment)”](https://hansard.parliament.uk/commons/1919-07-14/debates/2e37e143-49b7-4fa3-8a1e-155ab544b3b7/Women%28Employment%29) · [Royal Museums Greenwich, J. E. Bell’s Liverpool report](https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/objects/rmgc-object-475931)
- **Sex Disqualification (Removal) Act.** `HISTORICAL_RECORD` Royal Assent came on **23 December 1919 Westminster local time**. Section 1 removed sex or marriage as a disqualification for specified public functions, civil and judicial posts, professions and incorporated societies, subject to the Act’s provisos. Section 2 gave a route into the solicitors’ profession to qualifying women who had passed university examinations where degrees were withheld. Section 3 left universities free to admit women to membership or degrees. Cambridge’s later timetable shows what that discretion meant in practice. [Act as enacted, §§1–3](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1919/71/pdfs/ukpga_19190071_en.pdf) · [Parliamentary Archives on the Act](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/transformingsociety/tradeindustry/industrycommunity/collections/sex-disqualification-removal-act/sex-disqualification-removal-act/) · [Newnham’s Cambridge chronology](https://newn.cam.ac.uk/about/history/no-ordinary-rag-the-attack-on-the-clough-gates)

`HISTORICAL_RECORD` For the public explanation, Robinson’s spread supplies the visual sequence. Light from a distant star passes close to the Sun and changes direction. Projecting the arriving ray backward gives a slightly displaced **apparent** star position. The Moon blocks the bright solar disc during totality so the stars can be photographed. The observers compare those positions with reference photographs and test the size of the displacement. The report’s comparison was **zero, about 0.87″, or about 1.75″ at the solar limb**. The measured quantity gives the audience a way to read the diagram alongside the headline. **Myths / do not use**

- `HISTORICAL_RECORD` **“Spanish” identifies the origin**, or **Britain’s press was silent throughout**. The name reflects reporting and wartime conditions. The Cambridge articles show British reporting while the outbreak was still being minimised. [Bresalier](https://pure.manchester.ac.uk/ws/files/24146372/POST-PEER-REVIEW-PUBLISHERS.PDF) · [Cambridge summer transcripts](https://capturingcambridge.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/SpanishFluCambridge-Pt1.pdf)
- `INFERENCE` **A University shutdown, a pandemic quarantine on *Anselm*, or influenza causing the Madeira wait.** These would convert local atmosphere into unsupported expedition events. The school reports name borough and county institutions, and the route records explain the wait through the steamer connection. [Cambridge closure report](https://capturingcambridge.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/SpanishFluCambridgePt2.pdf) · [Trinity expedition account](https://archives.trin.cam.ac.uk/index.php/account-of-an-expedition-to-principe)
- `HISTORICAL_RECORD` **All adult women could vote at the first post-war election**, or **the professions Act gave Cambridge women full degrees at once**. Use the statute’s franchise qualifications and the University’s separate timetable. [Representation Act, §4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1918/64/pdfs/ukpga_19180064_en.pdf) · [Sex Disqualification Act, §3](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1919/71/pdfs/ukpga_19190071_en.pdf) · [Newnham chronology](https://newn.cam.ac.uk/about/history/no-ordinary-rag-the-attack-on-the-clough-gates)
- `HISTORICAL_RECORD` **Stars moved, light reflected, or the eclipse invalidated Newtonian calculations generally.** Apparent positions shifted. “Reflection” belongs to the Havas dispatch, and “overthrown” belongs to the Times headline. Einstein’s own explanation describes the close practical agreement with Newton’s theory. [Expedition report](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsta.1920.0009) · [Havas dispatch transcription](https://www.gov.br/observatorio/pt-br/assuntos/biblioteca/imagem/OEclipsede1919_ON.pdf) · [Einstein’s Times article](https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/weimar-germany-1918-1933/ghdi:document-4406)
- `HISTORICAL_RECORD` **Eddington hid the Sobral data.** The joint paper published the competing reductions and discussed the focus and scale problem. Later authors disputed the weighting, and a modern reanalysis supports the original treatment of the problematic dataset. [Expedition report, §§12, 22–25 and 37](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsta.1920.0009) · [Gilmore and Tausch-Pebody reanalysis](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/items/b89ca158-7c72-41aa-9c63-65785e31ac94)
- `INFERENCE` **A headline proves nationwide consensus, or Japan’s later crowds belong in I6.** The papers show differing editorial responses. The large Japanese public reception documented for Einstein’s visit belongs to **1922 JST**, outside this journey. [International press study](https://research.unl.pt/ws/portalfiles/portal/173097999/Sim_es_et_al._2026_.pdf) · [Kyoto Prefectural Library, visit-era newspaper and magazine records](https://www.library.pref.kyoto.jp/?pickupkiji=32451)
