## Answer

### 1. The documented human core

**Build the emotional climax around a man who hoped Einstein was right and still had to bring home evidence that other people could question.** Eddington’s Quaker faith gave him a reason to refuse military service. His scientific internationalism gave him a reason to take seriously a theory developed in wartime Berlin. The cloud gave him very little to work with. Those pressures meet in an ordinary action the player can share with him: changing a photographic plate on cue. [Matthew Stanley’s study](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/376099), [original expedition report](https://w.astro.berkeley.edu/~kalas/labs/documents/dyson1920.pdf)

**Currency.** Web sources checked 2026-11-27 JST. “Documented” below means supported by a contemporary record or identifiable firsthand account. Later recollections and scholarly interpretations are identified where they affect what the game can present as fact.

- **Conscience and the tribunal.** Eddington claimed exemption on religious grounds when conscription began. Cambridge secured an occupational exemption first, and his personal claim went unheard. During the wartime review, Cambridge reported that both observatory assistants had been killed in service, one in the explosion of HMS *Vanguard* and the other in France. Eddington was the sole staff member remaining. His own argument was Christian and reciprocal: he could see that men on the other side acted from the same patriotism and professed religious duty as his countrymen. On **1918-07-11 BST**, the tribunal accepted his conscientious objection and granted twelve months’ exemption conditional on continuing his current work. Dyson’s supporting letter emphasized the expedition’s scientific importance. The hearing record supports that distinction. A personal ultimatum to photograph the eclipse or go to prison would turn a later retelling into an official condition. [Tribunal press transcripts and Observatory Syndicate minutes](https://www.royalobservatorygreenwich.org/articles.php?article=1289), [Chandrasekhar’s later recollection](https://www.jstor.org/stable/531756)
- **Internationalism took work.** Willem de Sitter helped get Einstein’s ideas into British scientific circulation through the neutral Netherlands. Eddington studied and explained the theory and saw British testing of work from Berlin as an act of scientific cooperation. Dyson identified the eclipse opportunity, supported Eddington’s case, organized the observing effort through the joint committee, and helped analyze the Brazilian data. At Príncipe, Edwin Turner Cottingham, a clockmaker and scientific instrument specialist, controlled the exposures and attended to the drive while Eddington changed the dark slides. The report names Wright and Lewis, two Black Sierra Leonean cable technicians, as interpreters. Wright’s possible role in timing or calling plate changes is a scholarly inference. The observing site at Roça Sundy depended on infrastructure and labor within Portuguese colonial rule, including forced labor. The people and institutions that made the observation possible belong in the scene. [Dyson’s proposal](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/77/5/445/1272333), [Stanley](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/376099), [Simões, Soares and Carolino](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007087425000305)
- **The eclipse and the evidence.** On **1919-05-29 GMT**, a heavy storm preceded totality at Príncipe. Cloud persisted and apparently thinned in the last third. The team exposed sixteen plates. Only two, W and X, yielded the published Príncipe measurement. Some cloudier plates still showed a striking solar prominence. Eddington’s cable to Dyson, reproduced in contemporary *Nature*, was “Through cloud, hopeful.” The published deflections at the Sun’s limb were **1.61 ± 0.30 arcseconds** for Príncipe and **1.98 ± 0.12** for the Sobral four-inch instrument. Those uncertainties are historical *probable errors*. The blurred Sobral astrograph yielded roughly **0.93** or **1.52**, depending on the treatment of plate scale, and was set aside in the conclusion. The lower estimate was published. Historians later challenged the exclusion. Archival work and a modern reanalysis support the instrumental grounds for giving those plates little weight. The honest drama is that blur could look like a change of scale, while two teams working independently could give the result weight. [*Nature* cable notice](https://www.nature.com/articles/103265a0.pdf), [original report](https://w.astro.berkeley.edu/~kalas/labs/documents/dyson1920.pdf), [Earman and Glymour](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/philosophy/docs/glymour/relativityeclipses.pdf), [Kennefick](https://physicstoday.aip.org/features/testing-relativity-from-the-1919-eclipse-a-question-of-bias), [Gilmore and Tausch-Pebody](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsnr.2020.0040)
- **Private life and legend.** Eddington was unmarried. His sister Winifred managed his observatory household after their mother died. A colleague remembered him as retiring, while his letter to Winifred calls Cottingham good company and describes going on walks alone by choice. That evidence supports a private man with attachments. An account of chronic loneliness, a private romance, or grief for a lover would require invention. “Christopher Hayes” remains uncorroborated. The television drama *Einstein and Eddington* calls its love interest **William Marston**, a portrayal with no corroboration in the family and biographical sources consulted. The “third man” quip comes through Chandrasekhar’s later account of what Eddington told him about Silberstein. It can be presented as a remembered joke. Its exact wording is uncertain, and it supplies no basis for making the fictional assistant a secret third person who understood relativity. [London Mathematical Society obituary](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/LMS/eddington_lms_obit.pdf), [letter to Winifred](https://archives.trin.cam.ac.uk/index.php/letter-from-a-s-eddington-to-sarah-ann-eddington-18), [television dialogue transcript](https://www.stockq.org/moviescript/E/einstein-and-eddington.php), [Chandrasekhar](https://www.jstor.org/stable/531756)

### 2. Quiet moments worth staging

- **The house and the world are equally far away.** At Sundy, Eddington writes to Winifred that he has no idea whether peace has been signed, then asks whether Punch is getting walks and sends birthday wishes. He expects the letter may arrive close to the birthday. Give the player the letter after they have felt the wait for a ship or a cable. Let the birthday wish land on its own. [Trinity College, EDDN/A/4/8](https://archives.trin.cam.ac.uk/index.php/letter-from-a-s-eddington-to-sarah-ann-eddington-18)
- **He barely looked up.** Eddington recalled that the plate changes occupied him through almost all of totality. He remembered the strange half-light, the observers’ calls and the metronome. Teach that rhythm during a rehearsal. At totality, let the player glance up or keep their eyes on the dark slide. Give either choice the same historical exposures, with a different memory of the moment. [Eddington’s recollection quoted by Simões and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007087425000305), [original report](https://w.astro.berkeley.edu/~kalas/labs/documents/dyson1920.pdf)
- **The first result goes into a letter to his mother.** Eddington describes developing two photographs a night for six nights and spending the days measuring. The cloud had forced him to change his method. One plate agreed with Einstein, and he thought he had “a little confirmation from a second plate.” Let the player find the second faint image and decide how much confidence to put into their own notebook entry. The letter can then reveal how carefully Eddington described the same moment. [Trinity catalogue, EDDN/A/4/9](https://archives.trin.cam.ac.uk/downloads/reports/papers-of-sir-arthur-eddington-itemList.html), [Kennefick’s transcription in *No Shadow of a Doubt*](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctvc77fg5)
- **The poem gives him a dry sense of humor.** Trinity attributes to Eddington a parody of the *Rubáiyát* about the measurements. Its punch line is “Light-rays, when near the Sun, DO NOT GO STRAIGHT.” The date and place of composition are uncertain. Use it as an undated epilogue fragment. Its “light has weight” line belongs to the verse. The playable explanation can show a shift in apparent stellar position. [Trinity’s Eddington memorial](https://explore.trin.cam.ac.uk/assets/eddington/)
- **The announcement and the letter that follows it.** At the joint Royal Society and Royal Astronomical Society meeting on **1919-11-06 GMT**, Dyson presented the results and J. J. Thomson gave them exceptional weight. Silberstein still pressed the unresolved solar redshift question. The press amplified the result into a revolution. A quieter coda is Eddington having to write to Einstein again after the Royal Astronomical Society failed to confirm the gold medal he had already told Einstein to expect. Put the public headline and the private reversal within reach of the player. Both belong to the same aftermath. [Sponsel’s reconstruction of the meeting and press coverage](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007087402004818), [editors of *The Collected Papers of Albert Einstein*, including the medal correspondence](https://assets.press.princeton.edu/chapters/i7910.pdf)

### 4. Pitfalls

- **Turning physics into a morality play.** The expedition compared zero deflection, about **0.87 arcseconds** under a Newtonian-style calculation, and **1.75 arcseconds** under general relativity. Einstein himself had previously derived the half-value. Let the player see why the shift is difficult to measure and why the larger prediction matters. The record supports a change in how gravity is understood without making Newton or the skeptics foils. [Original report, §§1–4](https://w.astro.berkeley.edu/~kalas/labs/documents/dyson1920.pdf)
- **Giving the player a rigged triumph.** Let them decide what the plate supports, what caveat to record and whose help to credit. Keep the historical result independent of performance. Let Sobral’s sharper data arrive as relief after the island’s uncertainty. A gorgeous prominence and a plate useful for astrometry can be different kinds of success. [Original report](https://w.astro.berkeley.edu/~kalas/labs/documents/dyson1920.pdf), [Gilmore and Tausch-Pebody](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsnr.2020.0040)
- **Using a fictional bereavement to certify real conviction.** The assistant can witness Eddington’s choices and have a fictional emotional life of their own. For Eddington, the empty observatory posts, a letter about a dog and the caution of his first measurement already give the actor specific things to play. [Tribunal material](https://www.royalobservatorygreenwich.org/articles.php?article=1289), [Trinity correspondence](https://archives.trin.cam.ac.uk/index.php/letter-from-a-s-eddington-to-sarah-ann-eddington-18)
- **Making reconciliation too tidy.** The expedition could cross an enemy line while depending on an unequal colonial system. A public scientific success could coexist with a failed medal vote. Put those tensions in the work people do, the names they receive credit for and the letters they have to write. [Simões and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007087425000305), [Einstein papers editorial introduction](https://assets.press.princeton.edu/chapters/i7910.pdf)
